04   SOURCES & MODEL RATIONALE

How the estimate
comes together.

The calculator estimates beer and glassware mass. The arm-load estimates below are shown separately and do not affect the mass total.

01   The calculator model

Beer mass is stein count × beer volume × fill fraction × beer density. Glass mass is stein count × empty-glass mass. The total is their sum; tray weight and movement are not included.

Total load = beer mass + glass mass

Defaults use ±0.05 kg per glass and ±0.002 kg/L beer density. For each stein, the calculator adds the glass uncertainty to the density uncertainty converted by beer volume × fill level, then sums that margin across the steins. This is a conservative scenario range, not a statistical confidence interval.

02   Why these defaults?

  • 90% fill: an adjustable example, not a claim about every served stein.
  • 1.010 kg/L beer density: a modeled midpoint for an estimated 1.008–1.012 kg/L range. BJCP Festbier and Märzen gravity ranges inform the beer assumption; specific gravity and water density at 20 °C inform the conversion.
  • 1.32 kg empty glass: the midpoint of comparable 1 L Maßkrug product listings around 1.27–1.37 kg: Paulaner, Schweiger, and Stölzle Isar.

These product listings and beer-style ranges inform practical defaults; they are not a survey of every mug or beer served at Oktoberfest.

03   The biomechanics estimate

The count is split as evenly as possible, with an odd extra stein on the heavier hand. The forearm stays level as the upper arm swings ±25°, so elbow moment stays constant while shoulder moment changes. The combined load is treated as centered over the grip.
